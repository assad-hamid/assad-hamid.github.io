Crash Frequency and Injury Severity in U.S. Crash Records

NHTSA Crash Report Sampling System · Python · Pandas · Seaborn · Matplotlib

How do collision configuration, intersection context, and time of day distinguish crash frequency from injury severity?

I used Python to explore CRSS crash-, vehicle-, and person-level information, comparing the circumstances of recorded crashes with their injury outcomes. The analysis examined both event counts and within-category injury proportions to distinguish the concentration of crashes from the severity profile of those involved.

Analytical approach

The workflow used Pandas for categorical aggregation and cross-tabulation, with Seaborn and Matplotlib for comparative visualisation. CRSS imputed fields, including INJSEV_IM, NO_INJ_IM, and ALCHL_IM, supported the report’s injury and alcohol analyses; missing or unknown categories were handled according to each comparison.

Collision configuration differentiates injury outcomes

Front-to-front collisions showed the largest proportions of fatal and suspected serious injuries among the displayed categories. Rear-to-rear and rear-to-side collisions were dominated by the “no apparent injury” category. The distinction concerns severity composition within collision types, rather than their relative frequency.

Intersection burden and temporal concentration

Among intersection categories, four-way and T-intersections accounted for the largest displayed injury counts. Non-intersection locations carried the largest overall count. These comparisons identify concentrations in the analysed records without equating greater frequency with greater risk per trip.

Recorded crashes concentrated in the late afternoon, with the largest hourly count at approximately 17:00. The month–hour comparison also showed a recurring afternoon concentration, making time of day a useful dimension for describing crash occurrence.

Implications for safety analysis

The findings motivate complementary priorities: collision-specific severity assessment, closer examination of injury concentrations at common intersection types, and time-sensitive investigation of crash occurrence. Together, they provide an empirical basis for selecting questions for subsequent site-level assessment and exposure-adjusted modelling.

Analytical scope. These are descriptive patterns in the project’s analysed records. National estimates require CRSS survey weights; comparative risk and causal effects require additional exposure data and modelling.
